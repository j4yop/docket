Council review / Clocked Build Challenge / 6 Nov 2026

Docket can win Best Overall. The product is not the problem. The demo is.

Independent review against the official 100-point rubric and the public competitor set. Scope: product, demo, evidence and reliability. The marketing page is out of scope.

Verdict: position Docket as the authority-and-receipt layer for AI answers, not another document reviewer. Every other entry checks the document; Docket checks whether the policy behind it is still the policy — and records who released the answer. The field is crowded with focused reviewers; Docket is the only entry with policy authority, signed approval, human review and a hash-chained receipt in one loop.

The three P0 moves

P0 / 01

Make /tour one story, then breadth

Open with the France case: a support agent is about to send a draft; the newest global policy requires manager approval; the older France annex says it does not; latest-wins would publish the wrong answer. Let the gate resolve authority, contradict the draft, open a review and write an audit record. Then keep the eight-case suite visible as breadth.

Fix the defects the review found: the counter shows 8 / 8 while every row is Waiting; the meta line stays on “Starting…”; the “Why” column shows raw codes; partial runs need to render an honest Incomplete state.

P0 / 02

Make review honest

“Release answer” does not publish the answer. Approving a review resolves the record; the decision remains HOLD or REWRITE until the operator edits and re-runs. A judge can see “released by reviewer” while the unsafe draft is unchanged. Rename the action to Approve review; show Approved — re-run required to publish; put the governing quote and deciding claim above the buttons.

P0 / 03

Make the demo unable to fail

The deployed budget is 60 checks/hour per IP. One tour is 8 checks; four judges, retries and public voters can exhaust it. Keep a 60-check budget per session, raise the per-IP ceiling to 240–600, raise the daily cap, make partial runs credible, add a two-minute fallback video and a preflight for tour, console, ledger and health.

Blind spots the peer review caught

  • The scope rule can undermine the safety headline. The deterministic out-of-scope path bypasses Verify. It is defensible, but make the authority-trace determination visible, add a live scope example, and test a negative scope draft with a substantive extra claim.
  • The benchmark is self-authored. Keep 25/31 locked as the locked result and 29/31 post-tuning clearly labelled. The honesty is a strength; a hidden switch is a disqualifier in the judge's mind.
  • The first minute does more work than the feature list. Focused competitors win because the judge understands them immediately. Docket's depth must be legible before the judge scrolls.
  • The 60/hour budget is a single point of failure. The council splits on whether to spend time on it. The verdict: yes, but only half a day.

What would make Docket lose

  • A 429 during judging.
  • A first screen that still looks like a QA harness.
  • “Release answer” on an unchanged HOLD or REWRITE.
  • Presenting 29/31 as if it were the locked result.
  • A scope rule that looks like a benchmark-specific bypass.

  • Spending the four days on MCP or a ledger redesign instead of the first two minutes.

Advisors and peer reviews

Contrarian / Red Team — reliability, story, fake-seeming things

The highest-probability zero is the 60/hour per-IP budget. Lead with France, not the suite. Rename “Release answer,” expose Attest, label 25/31 and 29/31 honestly. Cut MCP, ledger redesign and the four-award spray. Strongest objection: reliability is invisible if the live tour works.

First Principles — authority resolver with a receipt

Docket is not a nine-endpoint checker; it decides which policy governs and proves the check ran against that text. Make the first screen a falsifiable contrast. Make the record the product. Spend the rest on reliability and reproducibility. Strongest objection: a fixed hero can look stageable and one-trick.

Expansionist / Strategist — own the authority layer

Every other entry checks the document; Docket checks whether the policy behind it is still the policy. Rebuild the tour around one supersession case. Enter four tailored awards, led by Verify/Compare/Rerank. Make the ledger the shareable proof object. Strongest objection: support answers read lower-stakes than contracts or invoices; keep all nine endpoints visible.

Outsider / Domain Skeptic — the first two minutes

The tour reads like a QA harness; the counter is wrong; raw codes. The console opens on a policy-authoring form. France is the one moment that lands. Make review honest; make the tour one story; put endpoint proof first. Strongest objection: review is the least visible fix; most judges never open it.

Executor / Pragmatic Engineer — ship in four days

Keep the existing runner, add a flagship first act, render the suite below, add the evidence strip. Rename the review action and state. Raise the budget or cap per session. Cut MCP, third-party corpus, ledger redesign, new attestation flow. Freeze code end of Day 3. Strongest objection: restructuring the working tour risks breaking it.

Peer review 1 — evidence and judge psychology

Strongest: the Outsider, for naming the “Release answer” trust bug and the visibility trade-off. Fatal blind spot: the First Principles advisor never addresses the 60/hour demo-failure path. Missing from all five: the rubric itself and the scope rule bypassing Verify.

Peer review 2 — execution and differentiation

Strongest: the Executor, for improving the first 60 seconds without betting the working demo. Fatal blind spot: the Expansionist leaves the authority receipt dishonest and overweights the ledger. Missing: an on-screen authority-trace explanation and a live scope test.
